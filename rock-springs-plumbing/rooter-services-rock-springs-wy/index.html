    Clay or older cast iron lines with trees nearby usually need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Newer PVC lines may go years without it. A camera helps set the schedule. Seeing how fast roots came back since the last cutting tells us whether to plan a yearly visit or stretch it to two years.

    Sewer pipes leak a little water vapor at the joints. Roots follow that moisture to the gap and push in. Once in, they grow quickly. In Rock Springs, clay with joints every couple of feet and old cast iron with lead and oakum joints get hit the most.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting works another way. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the pipe walls clean. Many tough lines do best with both, cut first to open the line and jetted second to wash out what the blades left.

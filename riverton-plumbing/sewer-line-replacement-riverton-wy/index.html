    How Pipe Bursting Works in Riverton

    A winch pulls the bursting head. The head is slightly bigger than the old pipe, so it splits clay or cast iron outward into the soil while fused HDPE trails behind it in one continuous piece. You get a pit at each end. The fused pipe has no joints, so roots cannot get in again.

    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Replacement in Riverton, WY

    Orangeburg is tar-soaked wood fiber. Many mid-century houses got it, and by now most of it has blistered and flattened enough to snag waste. Clay holds up better, except at the joints. When the camera shows trouble in three or more places, a full replacement is usually cheaper than chasing spot repairs.

    Sewer Codes and Inspections in Riverton, WY

    Inspectors check what gets buried. Checking local requirements before a project avoids surprises, and JKV Plumbing can confirm what permits apply to the work. They look at sizing, fall, bedding, cleanouts and how the new pipe ties into the city side. We book the inspection and meet the inspector. Unpermitted pipe sometimes has to be uncovered again for inspection, at the owner's expense.

    Upgrading Old Sewer Pipe Materials in Riverton

    Clay, cast iron and Orangeburg all fail in their own ways. Roots get into clay joints, cast iron rusts out along the invert, and Orangeburg fiber pipe squashes flat over the years. PVC and HDPE do none of that. Fused HDPE has no joints at all, so roots have nowhere to start.

    Fixing a Bellied Sewer by Trench in Riverton, WY

    A belly is a low spot that holds water. Bursting and lining follow the old path, so they keep the belly, and the only way to correct the fall is to dig, regrade the bedding and lay new pipe at a steady slope. Rocky ground slows the dig. We price that in after the locate shows depth. Each joint gets checked before the inspector sees it.

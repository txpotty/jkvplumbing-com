    Igniter and Flame Rod Repair in Riverton, WY

    Gas tankless heaters light with a hot surface or spark igniter, then prove flame with a flame rod. A rod coated in oxide reads low on a microamp meter and the board shuts the gas valve after a few seconds. We clean or replace the rod, check the igniter for cracks or weak spark, and confirm the gas valve opens on call. Five-minute fix sometimes.

    Recirculation Pump Repair in Riverton

    Recirculation cuts the wait for hot water at a far bathroom from a minute or more to a few seconds. A dead pump or a stuck check valve means you get long waits again or lukewarm water in the cold line. We test the pump, look for a thermal bypass valve under the farthest sink, and set the schedule so it runs around your mornings and evenings. Two settings, mostly.

    Condensing Tankless Repair in Riverton, WY

    Condensing tankless heaters pull extra heat out of the exhaust and make acidic condensate that has to drain somewhere. That condensate runs through a trap and, where code or the manual calls for it, a neutralizer filled with limestone or marble chips before it reaches the drain. Clogged traps shut the unit down. We clean the trap, refill the neutralizer and check the PVC vent for sag or the wrong slope.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Repair in Riverton

    Expect somewhere between $150 and $800 for most tankless repairs in Riverton, based on the part and the time it takes to find the fault. Sensors are cheap. A board or exchanger costs real money. We give a written estimate after testing.

    With yearly descaling, 15 to 20 years is common on gas tankless heaters. On hard water with no flushing, problems can show up in three to five years.

    Once a year on hard water without a softener. With a softener, every two years is usually enough, though we still like to open the unit annually to clean the inlet screen and check the flame rod. Very hard water may need it twice a year.

    Plan on one to three hours for a common fix like a flame rod or flow sensor. When the part has to be ordered, we usually return within a few business days.

    Hard water drops scale inside the heat exchanger every time it heats, and those passages are small. Skip the yearly flush and the unit runs hotter, loses output at high flow and eventually locks out on an overheat or scale code, which turns a routine visit into a heat exchanger replacement. The flush takes about an hour.

    Descale yearly on hard water and clean the inlet screen every few months. A softener or scale-reduction cartridge ahead of the heater slows scale a lot, and keeping the intake clear of leaves and lint heads off combustion faults. Check the vent cap after storms.

    Low flow at a single fixture, a dirty inlet filter, a failing flow sensor or scale will all cause temperature swings. A crossed hot and cold line at a single-handle faucet can do it too, which is a fixture problem, not a heater problem.

    Cleaning the inlet filter and wiping down the intake screen are fair homeowner jobs. Opening the sealed burner compartment is not, because a misaligned gasket or loose gas fitting there can leak exhaust or gas into the house, and many warranties are voided by unlicensed work. That work belongs to a licensed plumber.

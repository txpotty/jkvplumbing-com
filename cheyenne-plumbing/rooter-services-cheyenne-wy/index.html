    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Cheyenne

    Standard drain snaking in Cheyenne, WY usually costs $150 to $500 depending on where the clog sits and how easy it is to reach. Mains with heavy roots, or jobs that need jetting, run $400 to $1,000 and sometimes more. Camera work is extra. You get a firm price after the tech looks at the drain and before any cable goes in.

    Yes, most rooter jobs come with a limited guarantee. The terms depend on the line and what we found, and they are written on your estimate. Roots are the exception. A line with broken pipe or heavy root intrusion may carry a shorter guarantee, since roots will regrow until the pipe itself is fixed.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting works another way. A jetter scours the walls with water at high pressure. Many tough lines do best with both, cut first to open the line and jetted second to wash out what the blades left.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. Repeated use can weaken old PVC joints and pit cast iron. They also burn skin. A cable takes the clog out instead of melting a narrow path through it, which keeps the drain open longer.

    Sewer pipes leak a little water vapor at the joints. Roots chase that moisture to the joint and work their way inside. Inside, they thicken fast. In Cheyenne, clay with joints every couple of feet and old cast iron with lead and oakum joints get hit the most.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. Hydro jetting sends water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scrubs the whole pipe wall as it is pulled back toward the cleanout. Jetting leaves the pipe cleaner. Snaking handles one-time clogs, while jetting is for grease lines and scaled mains that keep coming back.

    We run RIDGID, Spartan and Gorlitz augers, plus sewer cameras and jetters from the same tier of commercial makers. These machines take daily abuse and keep working. The setup matters more than the name. A good machine with the wrong cutter can still damage a pipe, so we spend as much care picking the head as we do the machine.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. Heavy main line root cutting or a long jetting run can take 3 to 6 hours. You get a time estimate first. Once the tech sees the cleanout and asks which fixtures are slow, they can give you a closer estimate of how long it will run.

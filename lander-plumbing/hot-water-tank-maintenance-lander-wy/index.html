    Standard Tank Tune-Up in Lander

    A standard tune-up starts with the fuel off and the cold supply closed, then a full drain through the bottom valve until the water runs clear. Figure on half an hour or a bit more for the drain. We check the heating elements or burner, read the thermostat setting and look at every fitting on top of the tank for green crust or drips. Any weeping at the dielectric nipples goes on the report.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Lander

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. The new valve must match the tank's BTU or kilowatt rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. A capped discharge pipe is a serious hazard.

    Yearly Maintenance Plans in Lander, WY

    A maintenance plan puts your tank on the calendar so nobody has to remember. We call you when it is due. Each plan is a fixed yearly rate for one or two visits, priority booking and a parts discount. Homes on well water or very hard water usually do better with the twice-a-year option.

    Anode Rod Replacement in Lander, WY

    Water chemistry changes from one street to the next, and so does anode life. Three to five years is typical. A softened water supply can chew through a magnesium rod in two. Swapping a $40 to $80 rod is a lot cheaper than a new tank, and on a tank under eight years old it is usually the best money you can spend on it.

    Relief Valve Testing in Lander

    Temperature and pressure relief valves on residential tanks are typically rated to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees, and they can stick shut after years of mineral buildup. Our tech lifts the lever, lets water flow, then watches it close. A valve that drips afterward gets replaced. We also confirm the discharge pipe runs full size to within six inches of the floor or to an approved drain, which is what plumbing codes ask for.

    Emergency Tank Troubleshooting in Lander

    No hot water at all is usually a pilot, a gas valve, a tripped high-limit or a dead element. Those are fixable. With a leak, we first check whether water comes from a top fitting or from under the jacket. Leaks from under the jacket mean the tank itself has failed.

    Electric Thermostat Service in Lander

    Each element on an electric tank has its own thermostat, and the upper one carries a reset button for the high-limit switch. When the reset trips, something caused it. We trace the cause, often a shorted element or a stuck thermostat, before resetting it. Power stays off at the breaker the whole time.

    Electric Element Check in Lander

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. Each element gets an ohm reading. A failed lower element shows up as hot water that runs out halfway through a shower. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

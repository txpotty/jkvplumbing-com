    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Gillette

    A standard swap usually runs $500 to $1,200 installed. Add $800 to $1,800 for a battery backup system. Ejector pumps cost more, usually $1,200 to $2,500, since the basin is sealed and has to be vented.

    The primary pump runs on household current and does the daily work. A backup pump, battery or water driven, sits higher in the pit and only runs when the main pump dies, the power drops or the water comes in too fast. It is cheap insurance.

    A battery backup is a second pump, usually 12-volt, that sits slightly above the primary on its own float and runs off a deep-cycle battery. It kicks on if the primary loses power or fails. A charger keeps the battery topped off.

    Like-for-like pump swaps usually do not need a permit in most jurisdictions. Adding a circuit or moving the discharge line can require one, and every city handles it a little differently. We check the local rule before we start.

    Manufacturer coverage on the pump usually runs 1 to 3 years for plastic models and up to 5 for cast iron. Our labor warranty sits on top of that. You will see the terms in writing.

    Mostly cast iron Zoeller and Liberty units for primary pumps, with Wayne and Little Giant for some backup and utility jobs. Other brands are fine if they fit the pit. Plastic housings are cheaper. They tend to fail years sooner.

    Repiping and Pipe Replacement Options in Cody, WY

    PEX Repiping in Cody

    PEX installs quicker. Flexible lines snake through walls with fewer openings, and home-run systems from a manifold let you shut off individual fixtures without killing water to the house. Popular choice.

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Cody, WY

    We often leave old galvanized pipe in the wall. It is disconnected and capped, while new lines run beside it, so we avoid tearing open walls just to pull out dead pipe. Only accessible pipe comes out.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Cody, WY

    Galvanized steel was common before the 1960s. Inside, rust builds once the coating fails. Over decades the opening shrinks, pressure drops and the water turns brown after sitting, while threaded joints corrode through first. Replacing it is the only fix.

    Copper Pinhole Leak Replacement in Cody, WY

    Pinhole leaks start small. First a damp spot, then drywall damage. They usually mean the copper wall has thinned from corrosion, and when one shows up, more are often on the way along the same lines. Repipe or patch, we explain.

    Whole-Home Repiping in Cody

    Repiping sounds bigger than it is. You can usually stay in the house. We work room by room, restore water most evenings, and keep dust down with plastic sheeting and drop cloths, finishing with smooth patches ready for paint. Clean finish.

    Pipe Replacement for Multi-Unit Buildings in Cody

    Apartments and condos share risers. One leak can affect several units. We plan riser replacement in phases, one stack at a time, keeping each unit informed. Organized work.

    Drain Pipe Replacement in Cody, WY

    Drain lines fail too. Old cast iron rusts through. We replace cracked cast iron, failing ABS or old galvanized drains with PVC, checking slope and venting so the new drains flow freely. Camera first.

    Copper Pipe Replacement in Cody, WY

    Copper still has fans. It handles heat well and lasts decades. We install Type L copper for most interior supply lines, sweat or press the joints, and support every run with hangers at the spacing code requires, so the new pipe does not rattle or sag. Costs more than PEX.

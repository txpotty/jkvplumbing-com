    Jet Pump System Repair in Sheridan, WY

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Most failures start with an air leak somewhere in the suction line. JKV Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and watches it reach cut-out pressure. A jet pump that runs nonstop without reaching pressure has almost always lost prime or sucked air through a cracked fitting.

    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in Sheridan

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Most Grundfos calls involve a sensor or a drive fault. JKV Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Sheridan, WY well. Usually the electronics are fine.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Sheridan, WY

    Electrical faults cause a big share of well calls, from a tripped breaker to a burned contact in the pressure switch. After that, we look at the capacitor and relay in the control box. We lock out the power before testing anything. All wiring and splices meet Wyoming electrical code before the pump restarts.

    Well Pressure Tank Repair in Sheridan

    Bladder tanks hold a cushion of air that lets the pump rest between cycles. When the bladder fails, the tank fills with water and the pump starts and stops every time someone opens a faucet. We inspect the bladder, the switch and the tank connections. A properly sized tank can add years to pump life.

    Low Water Pressure Repair in Sheridan

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. Common causes include a plugged sediment filter, a low switch setting or a yard line leak. Sometimes the pump is fine and the well is simply producing less water than the house is asking for during peak use. We test each one.

    Deep Well Pump Repair in Sheridan

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up, run quietly and last, but motors, impellers and splices still wear out. After pulling the pump, we test each part and replace only the failed one. Heat-shrink splice kits and a torque arrestor protect the new work in your Sheridan, WY well.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Sheridan

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. The usual signs are no water at all, weak pressure or a hum with no flow. We test the motor from the surface first. When a pull is needed, a hoist brings the pipe up safely so we can replace the motor, the impellers or the check valve.

    ADA-Compliant Urinal Installation in Sheridan

    At least one urinal in a public restroom with more than one usually has to be accessible. That means a rim no higher than 17 inches above the floor, an elongated bowl projecting at least 13-1/2 inches from the wall, and a 30 by 48 inch clear floor space in front. Flush controls must be reachable. We mark it all during rough-in.

    Urinal Replacement and Upgrades in Sheridan

    Cracked or stained urinals are worth replacing. A hairline crack in vitreous china will eventually leak, and a bowl stained by years of hard scale often cannot be cleaned back to white, so a new fixture on the old carrier is usually the fastest fix. We check the carrier bolts first. Rusted bolts get replaced.

    Residential Urinal Plumbing in Sheridan, WY

    Home urinals use the same rules as commercial ones. The drain is usually 2 inch, the trap is built into the china, the fixture needs a vent within code distance, and the flush valve needs either a 3/4 inch supply or a lower-flow residential valve on 1/2 inch. Permit rules apply too. We check that first.

    Flushometer Urinal Installation in Sheridan

    Manual flushometers are still the most common setup. Manual valves go on a 3/4 inch line with the control stop tuned and the vacuum breaker at the correct height. Handle side follows the layout. ADA units need the handle within reach.

    Urinal Upgrades and Water Savings in Sheridan, WY

    Older urinals often use 1.5 gallons or more per flush. Replacing them with 0.5 or 0.125 gallon models on the existing carrier, and swapping the flush valve to match, cuts water use sharply in any restroom that sees regular traffic. Ask your water utility about rebates. We provide the model numbers you need.

    Office and Restaurant Urinal Installation in Sheridan

    Restaurant restrooms see a lot of traffic in short bursts. Vandal-resistant valves are available. Night installs keep you open.

    Waterless Urinal Upgrades in Sheridan

    Switching to waterless is mostly a fixture swap. The old flush valve comes off, the supply gets capped behind the wall, and the new waterless bowl goes on the same carrier with its cartridge housing tied into the existing drain. Many jurisdictions want a capped supply kept for later. That allows a future change back.

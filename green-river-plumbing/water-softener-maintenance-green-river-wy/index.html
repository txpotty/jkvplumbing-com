    Resin Bed Checks and Regeneration Settings in Green River

    Resin does the actual softening. Each bead trades sodium for calcium and magnesium until it runs out of capacity. We compare the softened hardness against the gallons used since the last regeneration to tell whether the bed is still at full strength.

    Breaking Up Salt Bridges in Green River

    To check for a bridge, we push a broom handle straight down through the salt. A hard stop, then a drop, means a bridge. We break the crust, vacuum out the salt mush below it, then check the brine well, safety float, brine line and grid plate before refilling to about half full.

    Softener Upkeep for Restaurants and Shops in Green River

    Ice machines, espresso machines, dish machines and boilers all scale up fast on hard water, and equipment warranties often require treated water. We test hardness on every visit and log it for your records. Brine tank cleanouts come around more often on these systems.

    Resin Rebedding in Green River

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water slowly swell and soften resin beads, and iron or sediment can coat them until capacity drops off. A resin cleaner is the first thing we try. If the bed is spent, we empty out the old resin and refill the tank with new high-capacity media, which runs far less than a new softener.

    Iron is the most common resin fouler we see. It coats the beads orange and blocks the exchange sites. We feed a resin cleaner through the brine well, run a regeneration and retest, and if the capacity comes back we shorten the time between regenerations so the iron does not build back up.

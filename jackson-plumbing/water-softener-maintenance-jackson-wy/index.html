    Resin Bed Checks and Regeneration Settings in Jackson

    Resin does the actual softening. Each bead trades sodium for calcium and magnesium until it runs out of capacity. We compare the softened hardness against the gallons used since the last regeneration to see whether the bed still holds its rated capacity.

    Salt Bridge Removal and Brine Tank Cleaning in Jackson, WY

    Humidity and cheap salt cause most salt bridges. A hard shell forms across the tank, and the salt underneath dissolves away, leaving a hollow. The softener keeps regenerating on schedule, but it draws plain water instead of brine, so the resin never recharges and hard water flows to every tap.

    Yearly Water Softener Tune-Ups in Jackson

    A tune-up starts with two hardness readings and ends with a full manual regeneration that we watch from start to finish. In between, we check the salt level, the brine well and the float. Figure on an hour for a typical unit.

    Control Valve and Injector Cleaning in Jackson, WY

    Brine draw depends on a venturi injector, a tiny nozzle and throat that pulls brine out of the salt tank as water rushes past. A few specks of grit can stop it cold. We pull the injector and screen, clean them, and replace the seals and spacers on valves like the Fleck 5600 and Clack WS1 when they show wear.

    Resin Rebedding in Jackson

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water slowly swell and soften resin beads, and iron or sediment can coat them until capacity drops off. We try a resin cleaner first. If the bed is spent, we drain the tank, pull the old resin and refill it with fresh high-capacity resin, which runs far less than a new softener.

    Breaking Up Salt Bridges in Jackson

    To check for a bridge, we push a broom handle straight down through the salt. A hard stop, then a drop, means a bridge. We break the crust, vacuum out the salt mush below it, then check the brine well, safety float, brine line and grid plate before refilling to about half full.

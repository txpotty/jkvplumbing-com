    Water Heater Line Thawing and Repair in Evanston

    Water heaters in garages or attics have exposed supply lines that can freeze. Tankless units are especially vulnerable. Freeze protection on tankless units needs power, so after an outage we check the heat exchanger and connectors.

    Slab Leak Detection for Frozen Pipes in Evanston

    Pipes under a slab rarely freeze, but the lines where they rise out of the slab at an exterior wall can. The riser is where trouble starts. Acoustic and thermal tools find slab-area leaks without breaking up the floor first.

    Pipe Insulation and Heat Cable Installation in Evanston, WY

    Insulation slows heat loss, but it does not add heat. Exposed runs get heat cable. We install self-regulating cable that adjusts its output to the pipe temperature, plugged into a GFCI outlet, and cover it with insulation so it uses less power and keeps the pipe above freezing on the coldest nights.

    Outdoor Faucet and Sprinkler Line Freezing in Evanston, WY

    Outdoor plumbing is the first to freeze, especially a spigot with a hose still attached. A connected hose holds water in the stem. Irrigation backflow preventers sit above ground with no insulation, and their brass bodies and test cocks crack easily, so we drain or cover them before the first freeze and repair them when they split.

    Freeze Protection for Commercial Buildings in Evanston, WY

    Vacant suites and warehouses freeze when thermostats are set too low. Property managers get a freeze plan. The plan lists shutoffs, at-risk runs and thermostat minimums so staff know what to do.

    Preventive Pipe Insulation in Evanston

    Foam pipe insulation costs little and prevents most freezes in attics and crawlspaces. It has to fit. We size the sleeve to the pipe, tape every seam and miter the corners so there are no gaps at elbows and tees, since a single bare fitting is often exactly where a line freezes first.

    Thawing Drain Lines and Sewer Pipes in Evanston

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Evanston

    Thawing an accessible frozen pipe in Evanston usually costs $150 to $400, and burst pipe repair typically runs $300 to $1,000. Prevention costs less. Insulating a crawlspace, adding heat cable to the worst run, and swapping outdoor faucets for frost-free models usually costs a few hundred dollars, which is less than a single burst pipe repair.

    A faucet that trickles or runs dry after a cold night is the most common sign. Frost on exposed pipe is another. A toilet that will not refill or a bulging pipe also points to ice.

    Thawing and small pipe repairs usually do not need a permit. Larger jobs can. Repiping, rerouting or replacing a backflow device may need one, which we handle.

    Check which fixtures work. Ice is usually between a working fixture and a dead one. If only the kitchen sink is out and it sits on an outside wall, the ice is almost certainly in that wall or the crawlspace below it, and a thermal camera confirms the spot without cutting.

    Most thawing jobs take one to two hours. Burst repairs take one to four hours. Repairs inside finished ceilings or walls take longer because we open the drywall, repair the pipe, test it and leave the opening clean and ready for a patch, and multiple splits on one run add time as well.

    You can open the faucet and gently warm an exposed pipe with a hair dryer. Never use a torch. If the frozen section is inside a wall, a ceiling or under the floor, call a plumber instead, because you cannot see whether it has already split, and thawing it can release water into the structure.

    Repairs carry a written workmanship warranty, with the term on your invoice. Parts carry the maker's warranty. It covers the repair, not a future freeze, so we recommend protection for exposed lines.

    A short, exposed section usually thaws in 30 to 60 minutes. Pipes inside walls take more time. Lines in walls or plastic pipe can take a few hours with gentler heat.

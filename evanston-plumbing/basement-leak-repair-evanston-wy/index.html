    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Leak Repair in Evanston

    Basement plumbing leak repairs in Evanston typically cost $200 to $1,500, depending on the pipe and how hard it is to reach. Pipe on an open ceiling is the cheap case. Opening finished walls or the slab adds cost, and a multi-floor cast iron stack can go past that range.

    Look for damp spots on walls or floors, a musty smell, and white chalky deposits on concrete. Those deposits are efflorescence, left behind when water evaporates. Peeling paint low on the wall, rust on the water heater base, and a water bill that keeps creeping up with no change in use all point toward a leak worth testing. Smell often comes first.

    A pipe or fitting repair usually takes an hour or two. Injecting a crack is a half-day job. Sumps take most of a day, and perimeter drains take two to five days depending on wall length.

    Plumbing leaks show up no matter the weather. Groundwater leaks track the rain. Turn off every fixture and watch the water meter, and if it still moves, the leak is in the pressurized lines, while water that only appears a few hours after a storm is almost always coming from outside.

    Crack repairs use two-part polyurethane or epoxy injection resin, depending on whether the crack is actively wet. Wet cracks get polyurethane. Our interior drains use rigid perforated pipe, which keeps its slope and can be jetted later.

    Check the base of the walls after a heavy rain. Dark damp bands, puddles at the cove joint or water near the floor drain are the usual signs. With everything turned off, a moving water meter means a plumbing leak. Note where the water shows.

    Shut off the main water valve first, then call us. Shutting off and draining outdoor faucets and irrigation lines before the first hard freeze heads off many spring repairs. We cut out the split section, replace it with copper or PEX, and pressure test the line before turning the water back on to the rest of the house. Insulating that run comes next.

    Yes, every repair carries a written workmanship warranty. Pumps and parts also carry the maker's warranty. Seepage warranty terms depend on the repair method, and we spell them out before you sign.

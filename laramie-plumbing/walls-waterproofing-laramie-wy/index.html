    Wall Waterproofing Work We Do in Laramie, WY

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Laramie

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and often add a dehumidifier to keep relative humidity below 60 percent. High elevation and strong winds make cold snaps harsher, pulling heat from exterior walls and exposed pipes. The joists stop rotting. Drier air under the floor also means less musty air upstairs.

    Sump Pump Installation and Repair in Laramie, WY

    A sump pump is the part of an interior system that actually moves the water. We set a lidded basin below slab level, install a cast iron pump sized for the inflow, and add a battery backup or water-powered backup for the night the power goes out during a storm. Check valves matter. A missing check valve lets the discharge line drain back into the pit and the pump short-cycles. The discharge line gets routed far enough out that it drains away from the house.

    Commercial Walls Waterproofing in Laramie

    Commercial basements, elevator pits and mechanical rooms in Laramie take on water the same way houses do, just with more at stake. One wet electrical room can close a business for days. We schedule around operating hours, work with property managers on access, and meet the waterproofing specs in the building plans for below-grade walls. Downtime stays short.

    Shower Pan Leak Repair and Waterproofing in Laramie, WY

    Wet baseboards outside the shower or a stain on the ceiling below usually point to the pan. We plug the drain, fill the pan with an inch or two of water, and mark the level and wait 24 hours. A drop means a leak. Rebuilding means a mortar pre-slope of a quarter inch per foot, a new PVC liner or bonded membrane, and a two-piece clamping drain set on the liner. We flood test the new pan before any tile goes down.

    Foundation Crack Repair in Laramie

    Crack injection takes about two hours per crack. We set injection ports every 8 to 12 inches along the crack, seal the surface with epoxy paste, and pump in resin starting at the lowest port and working up until it pushes out the next port. Polyurethane stays flexible if the wall moves a little, and epoxy bonds the two sides back together. The surface paste gets ground off once it cures.

    Plumbing Leak Behind Walls in Laramie

    A drip from a shower valve cartridge, a sweated copper stub or a loose drain slip nut can soak a wall cavity for months before paint bubbles. We find it with a moisture meter, a thermal camera that shows the cool wet spot, and a pressure test on the supply lines. Then we cut a clean access hole. The bad fitting or section of pipe gets replaced. Wet insulation comes out, and fans dry the cavity before we close it up.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Laramie

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most dependable way to stop a basement leak, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing so water has somewhere to go. Basements and crawlspaces are both common in Wyoming homes, and both need protection from freezing and groundwater. Backfill goes in last.

    Exterior Block Wall Waterproofing in Laramie, WY

    White crust on block or stem walls is efflorescence, salt left behind as water moves through the masonry and dries. The usual cause is a sprinkler head spraying the wall instead of the plants, or soil graded toward the foundation. We fix the water first. After that the wall gets cleaned, dried and coated with a breathable masonry sealer or elastomeric coating.

    Jet Pump Seal and Motor Replacement in Laramie, WY

    Single-stage and two-stage jet pumps wear out at the seal and impeller, and a leaking shaft seal shows up as a puddle under the motor. New pump, primed and tested. Then we watch the gauge through a full cycle.

    Pulling a Submersible Pump in Laramie

    Pulling a submersible means lifting the pump and every length of drop pipe out through the pitless adapter, sometimes hundreds of feet of it. We check the torque arrestor and every splice on the way up. Most jobs are done in a day.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Laramie

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Sand in the impellers and lightning surges are the usual culprits. Before pulling, we test the windings with a megohmmeter. That tells us whether the problem is down the hole or up top.

    Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Laramie

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes a healthy pump look sick. If the pump clicks on every time someone runs a faucet for ten seconds, the bladder in the tank has probably failed and the pump is short cycling itself to death. We check the air charge with a gauge. Precharge should sit about 2 psi below the cut-in pressure.

    Jet Pump Replacement in Laramie, WY

    Jet pumps sit above ground, in a well house or basement, and pull water up by suction from wells where the water level is within about 25 feet. A two-pipe deep-well jet can reach further down at the cost of efficiency. Lost prime is the classic complaint. Most of the time a bad foot valve or a suction leak is letting water drain back, and we check those before condemning the pump.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Laramie, WY

    No-water calls often end at the control box, not the pump. On a three-wire submersible, the start capacitor or the relay in that box fails far more often than the motor down the well does. We test them first. Swapping a capacitor costs a fraction of pulling a pump.

    Pump and Pressure Tank Matching in Laramie

    Pump and tank work as a pair. A tank that is too small or has lost its air charge makes the pump start dozens of extra times a day, and every start heats the motor. Every pump job includes a tank check.

    Constant Pressure Pump Systems in Laramie

    Franklin SubDrive and Grundfos SQE are the two constant pressure systems we install most. Both use a small 2 to 20 gallon tank instead of a big one, since the drive keeps the pump running at a matched speed. Soft starts also cut wear on the motor. The upfront cost is higher.

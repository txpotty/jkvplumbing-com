    Dealing with Grease Buildup in Laramie, WY

    Grease hardens as it cools. Then it narrows the pipe. Kitchen grease coats the sewer line the same way plaque coats an artery, and over a few years the opening can close to the size of a finger, which is when drains start to back up, and jetting is the right fix.

    Root Masses in Laramie Sewer Laterals

    Mature trees and old clay laterals do not mix. Roots find the joints. We cut roots in several passes, then look on camera to judge the damage and talk about lining or a spot repair.

    Tree Root Removal in Laramie, WY Sewers

    Roots get in through tiny gaps. Then they fill the pipe. A root saw on a sectional cable cuts the mass loose so we can pull it out through the cleanout, and a jetter can follow to flush out the fine root hairs left on the pipe wall, and a camera shows where they are getting in.

    Jetting a Sewer Line in Laramie, WY

    Grease and scale are jetting work. Cables leave them behind. A jetter nozzle with rear-facing jets pulls itself down the line and blasts the buildup off the pipe wall, restoring close to the full diameter of the pipe and slowing the next clog.

    Sewer Cleanout Installation in Laramie

    No cleanout means pulling a toilet. It makes every call slower. A two-way cleanout by the foundation gives us access toward the house and toward the street, so future service is quicker, and the work is done to local code with any required permit.

    Sewer Camera Inspections in Laramie, WY

    On a repeat backup, the camera goes in first. We would rather look than guess. A waterproof color camera on a push rod shows the inside of the line live on a screen, so we can see the clog, measure its distance from the cleanout and spot any crack or sag.

    Hydro Jetting for Sewer Lines in Laramie

    Jetting cleans the whole pipe wall. A cable only makes a path. A jetter sends high-pressure water around the full circumference of the pipe, removing buildup that a cable would leave behind.

    Cast Iron Sewer Cleaning in Laramie, WY

    Cast iron rots from the inside. Older homes may still have galvanized supply lines that narrow with rust and scale, causing low pressure and discolored water. Scale builds on top while the bottom channels out. Aggressive cutters can break through thin spots, so we use descaling chains and careful jetting made for older iron, which restores flow and gives a clear view of the remaining pipe wall on camera.

    Sump Pump Types We Replace in Laramie, WY

    Zoeller Sump Pump Replacement in Laramie, WY

    Zoeller cast iron pumps, the M53 and M63 in particular, are what we pull off the truck most often because they take heat and hard cycling better than plastic housings. An existing Zoeller can usually be swapped for a current model with the same 1.5-inch outlet and float style. Need more lift? We can move you up to a half-horse model like the M98.

    Pedestal Pumps for Shallow Pits in Laramie, WY

    Older basements sometimes have a narrow, shallow pit poured right into the floor, and a pedestal pump is often the only thing that fits without breaking concrete. We swap the pump, replace the float rod and stops, and run new 1.5-inch PVC to the check valve. Cheaper than a submersible, too.

    Combination Sump Pump System Replacement in Laramie, WY

    Combination units put a primary AC pump and a DC backup on one discharge manifold, so you get two pumps and two floats in a single assembly. They suit pits too tight for two separate pumps. We replace the whole assembly or just the failed side.

    Sizing a Submersible Sump Pump in Laramie

    Horsepower matters less than people think. For most houses a 1/3 HP pump is enough, provided the water only has to climb 10 to 15 feet to get out. Before we choose, we read the pump curve at your real discharge height. Oversize it and it short cycles.

    Elevator and Garage Pit Pumps in Laramie

    Elevator pits and parking garage sumps often require oil-sensing controls that stop the pump from sending hydraulic fluid or motor oil into the storm drain. Pumps and oil-sensing panels get replaced to code. Two alternating pumps on one controller mean one can fail without flooding the pit. Insurers sometimes ask for proof.

    Submersible Sump Pump Replacement in Laramie

    A submersible pump sits at the bottom of the pit, sealed in an oil-filled housing, and it runs quieter than anything else you can put down there. Out comes the old pump, out comes the muck at the bottom of the basin, and the new one goes in on a brick to stay clear of silt. The check valve gets replaced at the same time. Skip that part and the pump short cycles.

    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Laramie

    Stuck floats and leaking check valves cause more sump failures than dead motors do. When the check valve leaks, the water in the pipe drops back into the basin and the pump has to move it twice, which shortens its life. New valve every time. In a tight pit we use a vertical switch, and in a wide pit a tethered float.

    Light Commercial Sump Pump Replacement in Laramie, WY

    Light commercial work, think retail basements and small office buildings, usually means a 1/2 HP or larger cast iron pump with a 2-inch discharge and a panel alarm. We replace like for like or upsize. Grinder and ejector pumps are a separate job.

    Hydro Jetting vs. Snaking: Which Drain Cleaning Method Do You Need?

    By Aaron Mitchell · August 22, 2026 · 5 min read

    When a drain backs up, people just want it moving again. But the method the plumber uses makes a big difference in whether you see us again next month or two years from now. The two professional options are snaking, also called cabling or rodding, and hydro jetting. Both work, and both have limits. The right pick depends on what is actually inside the pipe, and in Wyoming homes that is usually grease buildup or root intrusion.

    How Drain Snaking Works

    A drain snake is a steel cable that spins as it feeds into the pipe. Small handheld drum augers handle sinks and tubs. Bigger sectional or drum machines with 50 to 100 feet of cable reach the main sewer through a cleanout, and the tip carries a cutting head sized to the pipe. A 4 inch main gets a 3 inch blade.

    Snaking is very good at punching through one localized blockage. A cable will break up a paper wad, hook a wipe or a toy and pull it back, and cut through a small root ball. It is quick and fairly cheap. It is also safe on nearly every kind of pipe. The catch is that a cable opens a path about the size of its head, so grease and scale on the walls stay put, which is why a snaked line can clog again within weeks or months.

    How Hydro Jetting Works

    Hydro jetting pushes water through a special hose and nozzle at high pressure, usually 3,000 to 4,000 PSI for a house sewer. A forward jet cuts into the blockage, and rear-facing jets pull the hose along while scrubbing the walls. Nozzles vary by job. We use a spinning head for heavy grease and a root-cutting nozzle when roots are the problem.

    Jetting does more than poke a hole. The goal is a pipe that is close to its original inside diameter, wall to wall. Grease, soap scum, sludge, sand and the scale that hard water leaves behind wash out toward the main. Because the walls come out clean, a jetted line usually stays clear much longer. The tradeoff is cost, plus the need to confirm the pipe can take the pressure.

    Hard, mineral-heavy water is common across Wyoming, and scale builds up quickly in water heaters, faucets and appliances.

    Comparing the Two Side by Side

    Here are the differences homeowners should weigh:

    • Snaking fits: single soft clogs, foreign objects, light root intrusion and a quick fix to get water moving.
    • Jetting fits: recurring backups, heavy kitchen grease, scale buildup, sand and silt, and heavy root regrowth after cutting.
    • Typical cost: snaking a main often runs $150 to $350, and jetting usually runs $350 to $800 depending on length and access.
    • How long it lasts: a snaked line may need it again within months, while a jetted line often stays clear a year or more.
    • Pipe condition: snaking is gentle on almost any pipe, but jetting an older or weakened line should wait until a camera has been through it.

    Why the Camera Goes In First

    Good drain cleaning starts with a look inside. The camera tells us if we are dealing with a soft clog or years of grease, or a root-packed joint, a belly or a cracked section. That decides the method. Jetting a cracked clay pipe can make the damage worse, while snaking a grease-packed line only buys time. A common sequence is snake to restore flow, camera to check the pipe, then jet to clean it all the way.

    Footage after jetting confirms the line is really clean. Once the walls are clean, cracks and offset joints that were buried under grease show up plainly on the monitor. Houses with mature trees near the sewer usually do better on a schedule. A root cut every 12 to 24 months, or a jetting with a foaming root treatment afterward, can push a big repair years down the road. Roots that return within a few months usually mean a separated joint, and at that point lining the pipe often costs less over time.

    When to Call a Professional

    Repeat clogs on the same drain, several fixtures backing up at once, or water rising out of a floor drain or cleanout all mean it is time for a professional.
